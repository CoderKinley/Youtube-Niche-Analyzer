A quick guide.

From a few keywords to a shortlist worth studying.

Find your niche

  1. Analyze a keyword.

    Choose a 7-, 14-, or 30-day window. Review the ranked videos and recurring phrases.

  2. Compare 2–5 niches.

    Use different keywords with the same time window. Select your saved searches, then compare.

  3. Study the top references.

    Export the top 10, watch them on YouTube, and note repeated hooks, setups, and payoffs before choosing your niche.

Connect YouTube

  1. Open Google Cloud Console ↗ and select or create a project.
  2. Under APIs & Services → Library, enable YouTube Data API v3.
  3. Under Credentials → Create credentials → API key, create a key and restrict its API access to YouTube Data API v3. Website/referrer restrictions do not work with this local backend; use an IP restriction only if it matches your outbound public IP.
  4. Paste it in API settings and click Save key. No restart needed.

The saved key is never shown again or stored in browser storage. Access is verified by YouTube when you search. You can replace the key from settings.

Read the signals

SignalWhat it tells you
Research scoreRelative strength within this niche’s sample, from 0–100. Scores from different niches are not directly comparable.
OutlierViews divided by the median views of up to 20 earlier uploads in the same duration class, selected from the latest 50. Fewer than 10 reduces the baseline contribution. Cumulative views are not equal-age measurements.
Average views/hour since uploadViews divided by age, with a one-hour floor. This is a lifetime average, not current velocity.
Small-channel breakoutScore at least 75, outlier at least 2x, 5 earlier same-class uploads, at least 1,000 views, at least 6 hours old, and under 100,000 visible subscribers. These are research thresholds, not YouTube rules.
OpportunityRanks performance evidence in selected samples using view rate, breakout frequency, small-channel hits, creator diversity, and baseline coverage. It does not estimate market competition.

This is a research score, not a prediction that the video will go viral.

Spot repeating phrases

We count 1–3 word phrases after removing punctuation and stop words. A phrase must appear in at least 3 videos across 2 channels, and is counted once per title.

Lift compares how often a phrase appears among flagged breakouts with its frequency across the full sample. No breakouts means zero lift. Frequency, creator count, and breakout count help break ties.

Scoring details

Research score: 35% outlier, 30% average views/hour, 15% engagement, 10% freshness, and 10% small-channel signal. Factors use tie-aware percentiles within each duration class. View-rate ranks also use age groups: under 48 hours, 2-7 days, 7-30 days, and older. Equal or singleton factors receive 50. The outlier contribution is multiplied by min(baseline count / 10, 1). Missing baselines and unavailable subscriber signals contribute zero.

Engagement: (likes + 3 × comments) / max(views, 1). Unavailable counts contribute zero and are marked partial. Ranking uses a 1,000-view denominator floor to reduce tiny-sample spikes; the displayed engagement percentage remains the raw rate.

Freshness: 100 through 24h, 90 through 48h, 80 through 72h, 65 through 7d, 40 through 14d, 20 through 30d, then 0 before percentile normalization.

Small-channel signal: views / max(subscribers, 1), capped at 10×, for channels under 100,000 visible subscribers. Hidden subscriber counts stay unknown.

Outlier: a zero median uses 1 as the denominator. The candidate and any later uploads never enter its baseline.

Opportunity: 30% median view-rate percentile among selected niches, 30% breakout frequency, 20% small-channel breakout rate, 10% independent breakout creators / total creators, and 10% share of videos with at least 5 earlier same-class baseline uploads. The old large-channel competition proxy has been removed. Compare matching duration filters and time windows using refreshed searches.

Keep the sample in perspective

Each search combines up to 50 popular and 50 newest matches, removing duplicates. Duration filters apply to that sample, so a filtered result can be small. Short candidates are videos up to 3 minutes; aspect ratio and actual Shorts classification are not verified. The sample does not represent the whole YouTube market. Rounded subscriber counts, upload age, and sparse baselines can affect the ranking.

Saved research keeps its original statistics. Reopening, sorting, comparing, and exporting it makes no new YouTube requests. New analyses use API quota; check your project’s quota in Google Cloud if requests are limited.

Channels and stats

Open ChannelsSimilar to find smaller creators, inspect median views, and compare repeat performance with one-hit concentration. Choose a saved search, duration class, subscriber limit, and minimum sample size. Unknown subscribers are excluded when a size limit is active.

Channel profiles include all collected uploads in the chosen window and duration class, even when their topics differ from your keyword. Typical metrics use uploads at least 24 hours old. Five measured uploads provide a developing sample; ten provide an established sample. These describe evidence quantity, not a guarantee of quality or future success. A top-video share above 50% is flagged as one-hit concentration. The latest-50 cap can leave busy channels incomplete.

Stats charts the discovery sample separately from channel upload histories. View growth uses repeated captures at least one hour apart, searching up to 30 earlier overlapping snapshots. Negative differences are shown as corrections. No background polling runs; Refresh research creates a new snapshot and spends quota. Reopening pages does not.

Older searches remain readable with their original scores. Refresh them for channel profiles and scoring version 2. Low competition, revenue, retention, and monetization eligibility cannot be established from this sample.

Find similar channels

The Similar page lists channels already found in your saved niche searches. Browsing, filtering, selecting a reference, and reopening a saved report make no YouTube requests.

Click Find similar to discover matches for a channel name, @handle, channel ID, or channel URL. Choose the correct channel if a name has multiple matches. Existing reports are reused by channel and duration class. Select the refresh checkbox only when you want new YouTube data.

Discovery reuses saved reference titles when available, makes at most two topic searches, and checks at most twelve candidate channels with twenty uploads each. It compares informative words in recent titles and shows supporting examples. A high similarity score means title overlap, not proven audience overlap, matching visual style, or low competition. Performance metrics remain separate from similarity. New searches use quota; no background discovery runs.
